# SM120 performance work: execution ledger

- **Date**: 2026-10-27
- **Basis**: `20261027-sm120-performance-research.md` and the probe round in `20261027-sm120-perf-probes/` (14/14 cells as expected, constraints in its `RESULTS.md`)
- **Rule**: one row at a time, in order. Every row is committed alone and reverted if any gate fails. The table's estimates are hypotheses, not promises; a row whose measured gain is <= 3% is dropped.
- **Status**: rows 0-2 and 5a-c landed; 13a landed on dense only; 14 landed neutral. The rest of the
  table is open. Clean-card result so far: dense 1.61x, sparse 2.34x, prefill 2.10x (see the
  re-baseline below). The next lever is the fp8 plane (row 3), which is what both the double buffer
  and a 512-thread CTA need - smem is at 100 KB of the 101 KB limit and registers at 254.

## Measurement protocol

- IOTA, **GPU 1**, `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1`, no other load on the card.
- Fixed A/B shapes (from the official perf cases):
  - `dense`: b128, s_q 1, s_k 16384, causal off, h_q 64, h_kv 1
  - `sparse`: b148, s_q 2, s_k 32768, topk 16384, d_qk 576, h_q 128
  - `prefill`: s_q 4096, s_k 65536, topk 1024
- Per cell: 5 repeats, report the median of `kernel_time_usage_us`; the official harness prints ms/TFLOPS/GB/s.
- Per-phase split (copy / QK / transpose / PV) via `clock64()` in a diagnostic build before row 1, so the Amdahl budget is grounded rather than estimated.
- Gains are reported as a multiplier against the row's own pre-change baseline, never against an absolute from another row.

## Gates (every row)

1. Full regression, EXIT=0 and zero FAIL lines: dense decode 2352, sparse decode 4748, sparse prefill 393, fused 8662, FMHA 16, API 3.
2. `out_drift == 0` over 6000 iterations per touched launch shape.
3. Two identical runs are bit-identical (determinism).
4. No regression on the untouched shapes of the same family.
5. Dense E4M3 smem rows additionally take an accuracy gate: max abs err <= 8e-4, cos >= the committed baseline, plus the `repro_dense_h_kv3_lse.py` reproduction.
6. Rebuild is always forced (`rm -rf build`, touch every `.cu`) - distutils does not track `.cuh` mtimes (§12.30).

## Baseline (row 0, uninstrumented build `a8d7961` + ledger, IOTA GPU 1)

Median of 5, `tests/bench_sm120_ab.py`, per-shape spread in parentheses.

| Shape | ms | TFLOPS | GB/s |
|---|---|---|---|
| dense (b128 s_q1 s_k16384 h_q64) | 9.806 (9.804-9.813) | 30 | 249 |
| sparse (b148 s_q2 s_k32768 topk16384 h_q128) | 79.131 (78.902-79.373) | 17.0 | 29 |
| prefill (s_q4096 s_k65536 topk1024 h_q128) | 72.096 (71.955-72.207) | 16 | 67 |

Baseline note: the research note quoted ~129 GB/s dense; the current build
measures 249 GB/s, so the estimator's multipliers are re-based here rather than
reused. Per-shape spread is <= 0.2%, so a row needs > 1% to be distinguishable
from noise on a single run; the 3% drop rule stays as written.

**Card contamination, found after row 14.** A leftover `dbg` process from the
probe round had been spinning on GPU 1 for the whole session and halved
throughput. Every row up to and including 14 was therefore measured against a
contaminated card; the row-vs-row ratios stay valid (same contamination on
both sides) but the absolute values are ~2x too slow and the *clean* factor is
not uniform (1.85x dense, 2.06x sparse, 2.03x prefill). Re-measured on the
cleared card, baseline vs current row:

| Shape | clean baseline | current (median of 5) | factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| dense | 5.313 (5.309-5.324) | 3.301 (3.279-3.331) | 1.610x |
| sparse | 38.350 (37.214-38.565) | 16.403 (15.947-16.699) | 2.338x |

## Phase split (diagnostic build, `FLASH_MLA_SM120_PHASE_TIME=1`, `clock64()`)

Per-block-0 `clock64()` totals over its full `batch` loop, normalized per page
and expressed as a share of the four phases (10 interleaved lines per shape,
fast-line values; stalled lines come out in multiples and are skipped).

| Shape | pages | copy | QK | transpose | PV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dense | 179 | 11.9% | 26.5% | 43.5% | 18.1% |
| sparse | 807 | 7.7% | 29.4% | 23.7% | 39.2% |

Consequences for the row estimates (Amdahl ceiling = 1/(1 - share)):

- Rows 1-2 target the copy, the smallest phase: a 2x faster copy is worth
  1.06x dense / 1.04x sparse. They stay, but at the low end of their bands;
  their real value is unlocking row 4 (a copy that can overlap at all).
- Row 5 (delete the transpose) is the single biggest lever on dense: the
  transpose is 43.5% there, so the ceiling is 1.77x dense / 1.31x sparse. It is
  promoted from "row 3-5 package" to first-class, and rows 3-4 are re-scoped to
  serving it (they shrink the PV operand traffic it re-reads).
- Row 3's dequant lives inside the sparse copy phase (7.7%), so its own
  ceiling is ~1.08x; its value is the smem byte reduction feeding rows 4-5.
- PV is 39.2% sparse because both warpgroups re-copy the V operand per segment;
  row 5 also cuts that (ldmatrix reads replace the copy+transpose).

Rows are ordered by risk-adjusted value. Row 3-5 are one interlocking package: row 5 alone on the current layout is expected to be near its lower bound, rows 3-4 make it reach the upper one.

**Re-scope after row 2 (2026-10-27).** Rows 1-2 both landed under the 3%
line (1.002x / 0.998x) for the same reason: the copy is a dequantizing copy,
so byte-copying it cannot pay on its own. Row 5 therefore goes next and is
split in two:

- 5a: `ldmatrix.m8n8.x4.trans.b16` on the existing bf16 layout. This is the
  form that works today and it deletes the 43.5% transpose on dense; no fp8
  needed.
- 5b: after row 3 has fp8 resident, the same read switches to
  `m16n16.x2.trans.b8` (probe 03's table) and the PV register copy disappears
  as well.

Row 3 must also keep V materialized as bf16 while PV still reads transposed
smem, which is why it does not stand alone either.

**Phase re-split after row 5 (2026-10-27).** With the transpose gone the
shares moved: dense is now copy 20.7% / QK 48.8% / PV 30.6% and sparse is
copy 11.5% / QK 36.1% / PV 52.4%, against 42.1k and 94.3k cycles per page.
QK runs on WG0 alone while WG1 waits, so it is the serial half of both
kernels - that is why row 13a (split QK over both warpgroups) went ahead of
the copy rows. Row 13a pays on dense (1.09x) but regressed sparse to 0.95x
and was reverted there: sparse already runs both warpgroups through PV for
52% of the block, so it gains less from the split and pays the two extra
barriers on a much shorter QK phase.

## Non-goals
