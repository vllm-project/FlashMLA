# SM120 performance work: execution ledger

- **Date**: 2026-10-27
- **Basis**: `20261027-sm120-performance-research.md` and the probe round in `20261027-sm120-perf-probes/` (14/14 cells as expected, constraints in its `RESULTS.md`)
- **Rule**: one row at a time, in order. Every row is committed alone and reverted if any gate fails. The table's estimates are hypotheses, not promises; a row whose measured gain is <= 3% is dropped.
- **Status**: rows 0-2 and 5a-c landed; 13a landed on dense only; 14 landed neutral. The rest of the
  table is open. Clean-card result so far: dense 1.61x, sparse 2.34x, prefill 2.10x (see the
  re-baseline below). The next lever is the fp8 plane (row 3), which is what both the double buffer
  and a 512-thread CTA need - smem is at 100 KB of the 101 KB limit and registers at 254.
  Row 7's accuracy probe is done: an E4M3 K plane costs cos 3.4e-4 / abs 1.2e-2 and can only land
  behind its own gate (abs 2e-2 / cos 1e-3), never inside the bf16 one. See "Row 7 accuracy probe".
  Rows 3+3b+12 landed **together at 1.10x** (14.86 vs 16.40 ms): they were reverted once,
  measured back to baseline, then re-applied, and row 12 more than pays for rows 3/3b by deleting
  the fp8 QK read's conversions. The package costs accuracy - the sparse kernel now quantizes Q as
  well as K - and rides the fp8 gate of §5. All six gates are green (see "Row 12"), so it stays.
  See "Decomposing the QK read" and "Row 12". Dense then took row 8 (TMA page
  load, 1.000x, kept for the code it deletes) and row 8b (slab-by-slab
  prefetch of the next page, 0.988x, reverted): the copy is the transfer, the
  overlap works in cycles and still loses on the wall. See "Row 8" and
  "Row 8b". Row 7 (dense fp8 K plane + `f8f6f4` QK + Q plane) was then built

  reproduces its probe, and the in-place conversion costs more in the copy than
  the atom returns in QK. See "Row 7 result". Row 6 (384 threads, producer
  warpgroup) was then the second negative in a row: the third warpgroup pins
  the register cap at 168 and pushes the accumulator into local memory, 0.216x,
  reverted. See "Row 6 result". A 512-dim NVFP4 single record
  (row 15) was accepted from a parallel session as a capacity row and is
  booked behind the open speed rows, not ahead of them. See "Row 15".
  Row 10 (sparse `tile::gather4` page fetch) was the third negative: the
  fetch is numerically exact, but the gather engine charges ~240 cycles per
  4 x 128 B op, so the copy rises 54% and the row lands at 0.92x, reverted.

  plane and put the cp.async copy back to show that only 4.1% is the layout and
  the rest is the gather engine staying off the LSU/L1: also flat, also
  reverted. See "Row 10" and "Row 16 result".

## Measurement protocol

- IOTA, **GPU 1**, `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1`, no other load on the card.
- Fixed A/B shapes (from the official perf cases):
  - `dense`: b128, s_q 1, s_k 16384, causal off, h_q 64, h_kv 1
  - `sparse`: b148, s_q 2, s_k 32768, topk 16384, d_qk 576, h_q 128
  - `prefill`: s_q 4096, s_k 65536, topk 1024
- Per cell: 5 repeats, report the median of `kernel_time_usage_us`; the official harness prints ms/TFLOPS/GB/s.
- Per-phase split (copy / QK / transpose / PV) via `clock64()` in a diagnostic build before row 1, so the Amdahl budget is grounded rather than estimated.
- Gains are reported as a multiplier against the row's own pre-change baseline, never against an absolute from another row.

## Gates (every row)

1. Full regression, EXIT=0 and zero FAIL lines: dense decode 2352, sparse decode 4748, sparse prefill 393, fused 8662, FMHA 16, API 3.
2. `out_drift == 0` over 6000 iterations per touched launch shape.
3. Two identical runs are bit-identical (determinism).
4. No regression on the untouched shapes of the same family.
5. E4M3 rows additionally take an accuracy gate. E4M3 cannot hold the bf16 tolerance (row 7
   probe), so an fp8 row is judged by **abs 3e-2 / cos 1e-2 / lse abs 2e-1**, and the bf16
   path keeps abs 5e-3 / cos 5e-6. `FLASH_MLA_FP8_TOL=1` applies exactly this set in
   `tests/kernelkit/compare.py` (it only ever widens). The lse term is separate because
   `log(sum exp)` multiplies S's error by the row's max. Row 12 raises the cosine term from
   the 1e-3 the row-7 probe suggested to 1e-2: the probe measured *K* alone, and K's
   quantization cancels in the suite (the reference reads the same cache bytes), while row
   12 also quantizes *Q*, which does not. Measured worst over the sparse suite: abs 2.1e-2
   (one element in 10.5M, a sign flip under near-cancellation), cos 3.9e-3, lse 0.10.
6. Rebuild is always forced (`rm -rf build`, touch every `.cu`) - distutils does not track `.cuh` mtimes (§12.30).

## Phase split (diagnostic build, `FLASH_MLA_SM120_PHASE_TIME=1`, `clock64()`)

Per-block-0 `clock64()` totals over its full `batch` loop, normalized per page
and expressed as a share of the four phases (10 interleaved lines per shape,
fast-line values; stalled lines come out in multiples and are skipped).

| Shape | pages | copy | QK | transpose | PV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dense | 179 | 11.9% | 26.5% | 43.5% | 18.1% |
| sparse | 807 | 7.7% | 29.4% | 23.7% | 39.2% |

Consequences for the row estimates (Amdahl ceiling = 1/(1 - share)):

- Rows 1-2 target the copy, the smallest phase: a 2x faster copy is worth
  1.06x dense / 1.04x sparse. They stay, but at the low end of their bands;
  their real value is unlocking row 4 (a copy that can overlap at all).
- Row 5 (delete the transpose) is the single biggest lever on dense: the
  transpose is 43.5% there, so the ceiling is 1.77x dense / 1.31x sparse. It is
  promoted from "row 3-5 package" to first-class, and rows 3-4 are re-scoped to
  serving it (they shrink the PV operand traffic it re-reads).
- Row 3's dequant lives inside the sparse copy phase (7.7%), so its own
  ceiling is ~1.08x; its value is the smem byte reduction feeding rows 4-5.
- PV is 39.2% sparse because both warpgroups re-copy the V operand per segment;
  row 5 also cuts that (ldmatrix reads replace the copy+transpose).

Rows are ordered by risk-adjusted value. Row 3-5 are one interlocking package: row 5 alone on the current layout is expected to be near its lower bound, rows 3-4 make it reach the upper one.

**Row 8 result (2026-10-28): 1.000x, and it corrects the row-4 reading.** 3.281
vs 3.280 ms, medians of 5, both sides rebuilt today on the cleared card. The
change does exactly what it says - 4608 scattered `cp.async` issues and their
per-token address math become 9 `cp.async.bulk.tensor` boxes, the plane loses
its 72-element pad (82.9 -> 73.7 KB) and both readers go through a SW128 XOR -
and the wall does not move at all. The re-taken phase split says why:

| phase (cycle/page, 179-page block) | before row 8 | after |
|---|---|---|
| copy | 8.7k (20.7%) | 10.1k (23.2%) |
| QK | 20.5k (48.8%) | 20.3k (46.5%) |
| PV | 12.9k (30.6%) | 13.1k (30.0%) |

The copy phase is *not* address arithmetic after all: it is the transfer, and
both variants expose it in full because nothing overlaps it. Row 4's "nothing
to overlap" reading was right about the buffer but wrong about the reason -
there is a 23% transfer stall to hide, and with the issue cost gone the stall
is now the entire phase. Row 8 therefore stays on infrastructure grounds (it
deletes code, drops 9.2 KB of smem, and is the base of 8b), and the value is
moved to **row 8b**: each slab is read by exactly one warpgroup in PV, so the
slab's own reload for page p+1 can be issued right after that read, from the
warpgroup that read it, behind a per-slab barrier - a ping-pong over the 9
slabs that needs no second buffer (bf16 cannot fit one) and whose ceiling is
the whole copy phase, 1.30x.

The TMA swizzle also cost a correction worth recording: TMA's 128 B pattern is
defined on the destination *byte* address, so in 2-byte elements the layout is
`Swizzle<3,3,3>`, not the `<3,4,3>` that GMMA's `Layout_K_SW128_Atom` carries
(cute scales that one through a bit-typed pointer flag). Hand-composing with
`<3,4,3>` and a plain element pointer silently produces half-right numbers
(cos 0.5).

### Row 8b (2026-10-28): the overlap works and still loses, 0.988x

Page p+1's boxes are issued from the warpgroup that read each slab, right after
that read, behind a per-slab barrier - a ping-pong over the 9 slabs with no
second buffer. The phase split confirms the mechanism: the exposed copy drops
10.1k -> 5.6k cycles/page. But PV rises 13.1k -> 15.2k (the issue and wait work
now lives there) and QK does not move at all, so 43.6k -> 40.9k cycles buy
nothing on the wall: 3.322 vs 3.281 ms, **0.988x**. Deeper coupling than the
cycle count suggests - the QK/PV reads now contend with an in-flight TMA burst
for the same L2/DRAM, and each slab's issue point stalls a whole warp.

Two deadlocks came out of it, both worth knowing:

- An issuing lane that waits for its own warpgroup's arrivals deadlocks: the
  reconverged warp sits behind the wait and can never execute the arrivals it
  owes (the case only shows up at b>=32; every small shape passes). Fix: the
  *last arriving lane* issues, so its own arrival is already counted.
- 128 arrivals per slab per page is 2048 smem atomics per page and cost ~1.2%
  on its own. A per-warp version (4 arrivals, `__syncwarp` + one arrive by lane
  0) removes that but deadlocked again at b>=32 in a way not yet root-caused -
  it is the obvious first thing to fix if this row is picked back up.

So 8b is reverted and row 8 stands alone. Dense has now been squeezed by every
copy-side lever the ledger has: 13a is the only dense row that ever paid
(1.09x) and the remaining dense structure is QK at 46.5% of the block, which is
row 13's territory (13a is dense-only; 13b/13c were reverted), not the copy's.

(no `Python.h` in `/usr/include/python3.12` on iota), `build_ext` writes only

**Ordering after row 12 (2026-10-28): row 8 goes next, ahead of 6 and 7.**
Rows 6 and 7 were both written as if the copy were a latency problem that
overlap would hide. Rows 4 and 12 say otherwise: the copy is instruction issue
(row 4 measured a two-stage buffer at 1.00x), and the SM is issue-bound, so
moving the same instructions onto a producer warpgroup (row 6) frees no slots
while the double buffer it needs still cannot fit in bf16 (146 KB). Row 7's
dense fp8 plane is the byte lever that unblocks both, but it differs from row
12 in one decisive way: the sparse cache is *already* e4m3, so its copy is a
pure `cp.async` of half the bytes, while the dense cache is bf16 and a dense
fp8 plane has to be built by a converting copy - LDG + scale + cvt + STS
against today's one `cp.async` per 16 B, into the phase that is only 20.7% to
begin with. Row 8 deletes the copy's address arithmetic and its 4608-issue
count outright (9 TMA boxes per page), which is what the phase split says the
cost is, and it is format-neutral. Probe 10's constraints fix the shape of the
row: 128 B-aligned destinations and 16 B-granular boxes, i.e. the 72-element
pad becomes 9 panels of 64 dims behind a 128 B swizzle, and only then is the
fetch a single `cp.async.bulk.tensor.3d` per panel. Rows 6 and 7 stay queued
behind it (row 6 still needs the bytes row 7 would free).

**Re-scope after row 2 (2026-10-27).** Rows 1-2 both landed under the 3%
line (1.002x / 0.998x) for the same reason: the copy is a dequantizing copy,
so byte-copying it cannot pay on its own. Row 5 therefore goes next and is
split in two:

- 5a: `ldmatrix.m8n8.x4.trans.b16` on the existing bf16 layout. This is the
  form that works today and it deletes the 43.5% transpose on dense; no fp8
  needed.
- 5b: after row 3 has fp8 resident, the same read switches to
  `m16n16.x2.trans.b8` (probe 03's table) and the PV register copy disappears
  as well.

Row 3 must also keep V materialized as bf16 while PV still reads transposed
smem, which is why it does not stand alone either.

**Row 3 result (2026-10-28): 0.90x, landed only as row 4's prerequisite.** The
plane halves (83 KB -> 42 KB) and the copy gets 20% faster, exactly as designed,
but the dequant moves into the two gemm reads and they do not pay for it:

| phase (cycle/block) | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| copy | 11.0k | 8.8k |
| QK | 19.3k | 36k |
| PV | 13.7k | 16.9k |

Both reads lost their vector width: QK now fetches a 2-byte k-pair per load
instead of 8-16 B of bf16, and PV's k axis is the *token*, which sits 656 B away
in the token-major plane, so PV reads one byte per element. Two remedies landed
and took the end-to-end from 0.78x to 0.90x: the per-atom scale hoist, and
splitting the QK steps across both warpgroups (WG1 idled before). A 16 B
per-atom load was tried and was worse (32 lanes hit only 8 distinct blocks).

The remaining lever is a byte `ldmatrix`, and it does not fit as-is: `m16n16`
**requires** `.trans` (probe 15), and the transposed 8-bit read hands each lane
4 consecutive *rows*, while every `mma.sync` B fragment wants 4 consecutive
*k*. PV's k is its row axis so the mapping would work there after a lane
exchange (LDSM lane `4*(l&3)+{0..3}` vs fragment `2*(l%4)+{0,1}`); QK's k is
contiguous and needs a 16-row group permutation of the plane instead. Both are
recorded as row 3b and not attempted here.

So row 3 was kept on the strength of row 4 only. Row 4 was then built and
measures **1.00x** (18.7 vs 18.6 ms, all gates green), so it does not repay the
10% and is reverted; row 3 stays because the reads are the recoverable part
(row 3b) and the plane is a precondition for any of it.

Row 3b landed as predicted and does not close the gap: the exchange is exact
(4748/4748, DRIFT=0, probe 16 exhaustive), PV drops 16.9k -> 15.3k, and the wall
still reads 18.5 ms = 1.005x. Rows 3, 3b and 4 were then all reverted and the
tree re-measured at 16.20 ms against the 16.40 ms clean baseline, i.e. the revert
really does restore the baseline.

### Decomposing the QK read (why 3b could not win, and what is left)

A re-taken phase split on the fp8 + 3b tree puts the loss where 3b cannot reach
it:

| phase (cycle/block, 512-page arm) | bf16 baseline | fp8 + 3b |
|---|---|---|
| copy | 11.0k | 7.5k |
| QK | 19.3k | 27.4k |
| PV | 13.7k | 15.3k |
| total | 44.0k | 50.2k |

QK's per-16-dim step is 8 `LDS.128` + per atom 2 conversions and 2 multiplies
against bf16's 16 plain loads: 16 instructions become ~56, and that - not byte
volume - is what the 8k cycles are. Two consequences:

- The multiplier cannot be hoisted out of the loop: the scale is per (token,
  quant tile) and rP *accumulates* across segments, so scaling the accumulator
  instead would cost 64 muls per segment against the current 16.
- The conversions can be deleted outright by feeding the mma raw e4m3
  (`mma.sync.kind::f8f6f4`, row 12). Then a B fragment is 4 bytes fetched by one
  `LDS.32` - half of the bf16 load count - with no cvt and no multiply, and Q
  carries the only quantization left. That is the row that decides whether the
  fp8 plane survives at all.

Rows 3 and 3b therefore go back into the tree as row 12's base, at a known
0.90x, and row 12 is judged on the *pair*: if it does not take the package to
1.03x or better, the whole thing is reverted for good.

### Row 12 (2026-10-28): 1.10x, the conversions go away and Q pays for it

`mma.sync.kind::f8f6f4` takes the raw e4m3 bytes of both operands, so a B
fragment is two 4-byte `LDS.32` at `4*(t%4)` and `+16` (the atom's own
MMA_Traits layout, verified against `cute::copy` bit for bit) and the 16
conversions plus 16 multiplies per 16 dims go to zero. Q has to become e4m3 too
and is quantized once per batch into a 34 KB plane, one row-scale per 128-dim
tile; its scale rides the same flush as the token scale, so the inner loop has
no arithmetic but the gemm.

Gates, all six: dense decode 2352, sparse decode 4748, sparse prefill 393,
fused 8662, FMHA 16, API 3 - every suite EXIT=0, zero FAIL. Drift 0/6000 dense,
0/6000 sparse, 0/2000 prefill - the drift script reruns on identical inputs and
compares every bit against the first run, so DRIFT=0 is the determinism gate
(gate 3) as well. Dense decode re-ran under the
*un-widened* bf16 gate and still passes, so the dense kernel is untouched in
accuracy as well as in code. The suite now carries §5's numbers itself
(`665a036`), so a green sparse run means the fp8 gate and not the bf16 one.

14.86 ms (14.75-14.93) against 16.40: **1.10x**, DRIFT=0 over 6000. The
package's first real win, and it is worth more than the 3b that fed it.

Two consequences worth recording:

- **The Q plane does not fit next to the 64 KB O staging buffer** (99 KB / SM
  here, 101376 opt-in). The epilogue now stages O in halves of 256 V dims and
  stores them back to back, which is free because it runs once per batch; the
  descriptor box drops to 4 chunks and the store coordinate picks the half.
  This is also what unblocks a wider CTA later - the plan is at 94 KB.
- **Accuracy**: quantizing Q is not free the way quantizing K is. K's error
  cancels in the suite (the reference reads the same fp8 cache), Q's does not,
  and it lands at abs 2.1e-2 / cos 3.9e-3 / lse 0.10 worst case across the
  sparse suite - hence §5's own gate. Scale granularity is not a lever (row
  scale and per-128-tile scale agree to four digits, as the row 7 probe said),
  so the only way back to the bf16 gate would be a bf16 A operand, i.e. row 3's
  slower read.


Why the overlap is worth nothing: the copy phase is **address arithmetic**, not
memory latency. Per page and thread it is ~10 `cp.async` issues behind a runtime
integer division (`token_index / page_block_size`), a `%`, and a per-dim address
expression; the loads themselves complete long before the barrier. Prefetching
the next page one buffer ahead removes a stall that was never there. The row
would pay only after the copy's own instruction count comes down, which is a
different row.

Row 4 also cost 84 KB of the smem budget (two 42 KB planes), which is why the
lse partial `sP` had to move from fp32 to fp16 on the way (fp32 pushed the plan
to 104.5 KB and the launch failed; fp16 lands at 96 KB and keeps the 1.2e-4 gate,
where bf16's 2e-4 does not).

**Row 7 conversion-cost probe (2026-10-28, `da1cf51`).** Row 8 changed the
copy, so row 7's old pricing (a converting copy against cp.async) no longer
applies: the copy is now a TMA transfer with the SM idle behind it, and the
only open question was whether a conversion can ride that stall. It cannot.
`FLASH_MLA_SM120_QUANT_PROBE=1` reads every element as a converting copy would
(16 B chunk, per-token scale, e4m3), writes nothing back. Interleaved against
its own baseline it is 0.978x, i.e. the conversion costs **2.2%** - the 6% the
first, unpaired run suggested was mostly card drift.

`FLASH_MLA_SM120_QK8_PROBE=1` cuts QK's B-fragment loads to 1/8 (values wrong)
to price the read the fp8 atom would replace: **1.002x**, nothing. Dense QK is
not read-bound - unlike sparse, its plane is already bf16, so rows 3/3b never
put a cvt and a multiply on every load, and the 16 plain loads per atom are
free. QK is nonetheless 46.5% of this block, so what it is bound by is the mma
itself. That is where `f8f6f4` actually pays: its k is 32, half the bf16 atom's,
so the same 64-dim segment costs half the mma instructions.

`FLASH_MLA_SM120_QKMMA_PROBE=1` halves that mma (9 x 32 dims instead of 9 x 64):
**1.032x**. So QK is bound by neither its reads nor its math either - and the
third probe finds what it *is* bound by. Dropping Q's per-segment global
re-read (`FLASH_MLA_SM120_QPROBE`) is **1.185x**: dense re-reads the whole 64 x
576 Q tile from L2 once per segment *per page*, 73.7 KB of traffic per page,
and that - not the gemm - is what fills the QK phase. It is also exactly what
row 12 deletes, by quantizing Q once per batch into an smem plane.

Row 7 is therefore priced on the Q plane, with the fp8 atom as the thing that
makes the plane cheap to read (4 B per atom from smem against a global re-read)
and 2.2% of conversion as the price. The three levers are additive in the wrong
order - Q plane 18.5%, atom 3.2%, conversion -2.2% - so the row starts with the
Q plane and the atom, and the K plane conversion is what buys the atom.

**Row 7 accuracy probe (2026-10-28).** `tests/quant_feasibility_sm120.py`
pushes K through the exact quantize -> dequantize an E4M3 plane would do, then
runs today's kernel on it and compares against the torch reference over the six
fixed dense cases. The control row (same path, no quantizing) passes with cos
8e-9-2.3e-6, i.e. the kernel's own noise already spends up to 45% of the 5e-6
cosine budget.

| K quantized | worst cos | worst abs | vs gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| none (control) | 2.3e-6 | 1.95e-3 | pass |
| rope 64 dims only | 2.3e-6 | 1.95e-3 | pass - rope is free |
| nope 512 dims, per tile | 3.4e-4 | 1.17e-2 | fail, 65x the cos gate |
| nope 512 dims, per token / per page | 3.7e-4 | 1.37e-2 | fail - granularity is not the lever |
| nope 512 dims, per tile + 64-pt Hadamard | 3.6e-4 | 1.01e-2 | fail - no cos gain, abs only 15% better |

Two conclusions:

1. The error is the E4M3 mantissa itself, not outliers and not the scale
   granularity: a per-token scale is no better than a per-64-dim tile, and the
   Hadamard rotation that normally absorbs outliers changes the cosine not at
   all.
2. The bf16 gate is not reachable on an fp8 plane. A viable gate needs abs
   2e-2 / cos 1e-3 (all six cases pass, 1.7x / 3x headroom; abs 1e-2 fails 3 of
   6). So row 7 lands as an **opt-in fp8 mode with its own documented gate**,
   not as a drop-in replacement.

The value is unchanged - half the K bytes is what both the double buffer and a
512-thread CTA are waiting on - but the gate the row is judged by changes: for
row 7 the accuracy gates above become abs 2e-2 / cos 1e-3, and the bf16 path
keeps 5e-3 / 5e-6.

**Row 7 result (2026-10-29): built and measured five ways, 0.92x, reverted.** The accuracy
half of the row works exactly as probed (out cos 3.2-3.4e-4, abs 1.1e-2, lse cos
7e-7 on all three probe shapes) - the row does not die on tolerance. It dies on
time, and on a base the probes no longer describe: row 8 rewrote the page copy
to TMA and deleted row 12's fp8 QK with it, so row 7 had to re-earn the atom and
pay for a conversion the probe never included. Interleaved, medians of 5:
3.712 vs 3.402 ms. Phase split of that build: copy 10.1k -> 12.4k cycle/page
(the in-place conversion and its barriers), QK 20.6k -> 21.5k, PV
14.1k -> 13.9k. **The fp8 atom is not a win here**: halving QK's mma and its
A-operand bytes moved QK by +0.9k, so the 1.185x Q probe and the 1.032x mma
probe both fail to reproduce once the operands are free - QK sits on the
mma/softmax latency chain and on neither operand.

Three further builds tried to buy the conversion back:

1. One barrier per slab, all 18 boxes in flight, so the conversion rides the
   remaining transfer instead of following it: **4.07 ms**. The nine extra waits
   cost more than the overlap saves - the same lesson as row 8b.
2. A Q plane in the page buffer's tail (the span the in-place plane never
   reaches), re-quantized per page because the TMA clobbers it, with the amax
   hoisted to once per request: **5.74 ms**. Much worse than the gmem Q re-read
   it replaces, and the copy phase alone goes to 36k cycle/page; gating the
   conversion to the valid q rows (8 tiles instead of 512 per page for a decode
   batch) recovers almost nothing, so the cost is not the element work.
3. A 16 B Q row pad to break the bank period did not move it either.

Two dead ends inside the attempt are worth keeping: the TMA box is the whole
64-token slab, so splitting a slab into two 32-token boxes issues 2x the
transfer and makes the barrier's `expect_tx` fire half way through the page; and
a smem Q plane has to serve both QK's fragments and its own quantization, where
a 512 B row stride puts every row on the same banks.

Row 7 closes as a negative. Its premise - that QK is fed by Q's re-read and
that a shorter atom shortens QK - no longer holds on the TMA base: the copy the
plane needs costs more than any operand it takes away, and every shape of the
row loses by 8% or more. The accuracy result stands and is what an opt-in fp8
mode would be built on if a later row makes the copy free.

**Row 6 result (2026-10-29): 0.216x, reverted - the third warpgroup does not
fit the register cap.** 3.40 -> 15.81 ms, medians of 5, interleaved, both
sides built from the same HEAD on the cleared card. Built exactly as
specified: 384 threads, WG2 = producer (the page's TMA issue and the tail
zero-fill), `setmaxnreg` 40 dealloc / 232 alloc, and every math touch of
rO/rP/rS gated so that a producer store cannot land in the next warp's
registers.

The failure is mechanical and worth keeping: **ptxas compiles to the launch
bound, not to the setmaxnreg target.** 384 threads pin the cap at
65536/384 = 168; the math path wants 255, of which rO alone is 128 accumulator
registers. The build reports 168 registers with 3304 B spill stores / 3428 B
spill loads (from 28/32), a 1344 B stack (from 544) and 46% more SASS (380 ->
1379 LDL/STL). The cause is visible directly: the four rO segments are zeroed
with 32 consecutive `STL.128` into the local frame, so PV reads and rewrites
its accumulator through local memory on every page. `USETMAXREG.DEALLOC 0x28`
and `.TRY_ALLOC 0xe8` are emitted, so the reconfiguration does happen - it just
has nothing to give back, because a warp can only address the registers ptxas
assigned it statically.

| phase (cycle/page, 179-page block) | base | row 6 |
|---|---|---|
| copy | 10.1k | 42.5k |
| QK | 20.3k | 102.9k |
| PV | 13.1k | 53.4k |

Everything degrades together, by 3.7-5.1x, and QK degrades most - not a
producer serialization but the cost of every fragment and accumulator going
through the local frame.

The row's premise was the research note's "our 168 regs survive this split
better than a naive CTA-wide bump", read off the FlashInfer ncu rather than our
kernel. Ours runs at 255 with 28 B of spill, so the cap is already saturated
and a wider CTA can only trade registers away. What the producer would buy is
small on the TMA base in any case - row 8 reduced the copy to nine issues from
one thread, which is also why 8b could not convert. A 288-thread CTA with a
single producer warp (cap 227) is the only remaining shape, but `setmaxnreg` is
a warpgroup-wide `.sync.aligned` op and 288 threads leave warpgroup 2 with one
warp; the cap would still be 28 registers short of the accumulators.

Consequence for later rows: 256 threads is as wide as this CTA goes until the
accumulator shrinks - anything that wants a fourth role, or 2 CTA/SM, has to
buy the registers back first (e.g. keep rO in smem between segments).

**Row 10 result (2026-10-29): 0.92x, reverted - the gather engine is priced
per operation, not per issue.** 15.55 vs 14.33 ms,
medians of 5, interleaved; the per-pair ratio never leaves 0.917-0.939. The
`tile::gather4` page fetch is numerically *exact* against HEAD (V4 bit-for-bit
at maxerr 2.56e-3, fp8 cos 0.999069 / 0.999112 at maxerr 2.6e-3, `out_drift`
0), so the row was lost purely on speed. The phase split says where:

| phase (cycle/page) | base | row 10 |
|---|---|---|
| copy | 10.1k | 15.6k |
| QK | 15.4k | 13.0k |
| barrier | 50 | 51 |
| PV | 16.4k | 16.8k |

The copy hypothesis was "4608 scattered `cp.async` issues become 64 TMA
descriptor ops". The count did fall 72x and the copy got 54% *slower*: a page
is 64 gather ops of 4 x 128 B each, and an op costs ~240 cycles of its own -
the engine walks four unrelated blocks' addresses per instruction, which is
scatter *latency* hardware, not a bulk copier. The transfer is byte-identical
to base, so the whole +5.4k is engine and issue cost; that the 64 ops are
issued by 2 warps of WG0 while 6 warps idle makes it worse but is not the
cause. Against that, QK dropped 16%: the 4 x 128 B swizzled plane the gather
writes is materially better for the `b8` fragment reads than the 768 B row
plane base uses. That is the one part of row 10 worth re-buying - the same
plane layout fed by the old `cp.async` copy - and it is worth a probe because
QK is 37% of this (compute-bound) block. It is a phase attribution from one
diagnostic pair, not an isolated A/B, so a later row has to re-earn it.
**Row 16 took exactly that probe and the attribution was mostly the engine:
with the same plane filled by `cp.async`, only 4.1% of the 16% survives.** See
"Row 16 result".

Interface facts measured for the probes, all of which cost the row a build:
`tile::gather4` is 2D-only - ptxas rejects `.3d` on it (probe 14); the y
stride of the descriptor must be a multiple of 16, so a 1-byte axis is refused
with `CUDA_ERROR_INVALID_VALUE` (probe 15) and the row axis has to be a token
index with a 576/656 B stride; the 128 B swizzle phase is defined on the
absolute destination smem address, not the box (probe 11). Two loader bugs had
to be fixed before anything was comparable: V4's 8 scale bytes per token do not
sit in the token's row - `quantize_k_cache` puts them in a per-block tail - so
a V4 map row is exactly the 576 B data region and a block's byte count is
padded to a 576 multiple, which is one row more than the page: that tail. A
flat token axis is therefore off by one row per block, and the coordinate has
to be `y = block*(block_bytes/row_bytes) + rel`, the row unit pinned to V4's
576 data bytes and to every other format's whole token; and
the four gathered tokens are four *arbitrary* tokens, so each needs its own
block and rel - the loader thread's own `token_index` is only right for its
own row, which was the NaN. Probes 11-15 stay in the probes directory and the
loader change is archived as
`20261027-sm120-perf-probes/row10_gather4_loader.patch`.

**Environment trap from row 10:** the build goes through `ccache`, and
`NVCC_PREPEND_FLAGS` is expanded inside `nvcc`, where ccache's hash cannot see
it. A diagnostic build made by only changing that variable is served entirely
from cache - ptxas `-v` output and all - and silently lacks the new define.
Set `CCACHE_DISABLE=1` for any flag-only rebuild.

**Phase re-split after row 5 (2026-10-27).** With the transpose gone the
shares moved: dense is now copy 20.7% / QK 48.8% / PV 30.6% and sparse is
copy 11.5% / QK 36.1% / PV 52.4%, against 42.1k and 94.3k cycles per page.
QK runs on WG0 alone while WG1 waits, so it is the serial half of both
kernels - that is why row 13a (split QK over both warpgroups) went ahead of
the copy rows. Row 13a pays on dense (1.09x) but regressed sparse to 0.95x
and was reverted there: sparse already runs both warpgroups through PV for
52% of the block, so it gains less from the split and pays the two extra
barriers on a much shorter QK phase.

## Row 16 result (2026-10-29): 1.00x, reverted - and row 10's QK gain was the engine, not the plane

The layout is separated from the engine and the separation answers the
question: 14.55 vs 14.79 ms (medians of 5, interleaved) with the variant
winning 2 pairs and losing 3, mean ratio 0.9997x - flat. Confirmed on the
phases, which are the interesting part:

| phase (cycle/page) | base | row 10 (gather) | row 16 (cp.async) |
|---|---|---|---|
| copy | 10.1k | 15.6k | 10.3k |
| QK | 15.4k | 13.0k | 14.8k |
| PV | 16.4k | 16.8k | 16.7k |

Same plane, same reader, same contents as row 10 - QK gives up 2.4k -> 0.6k
of its gain (16% -> 4.1%) purely from the producer change. So the "layout the
gather writes is what made QK faster" reading in row 10 was mostly wrong: the
bytes are identical at the destination, and what the gather also did was keep
the copy off the LSU and the L1 (`.ca` here pulls every 16 B line through the
lane's cache path, TMA does not), which is what QK's gmem Q re-read feels. The
layout itself is worth ~4% of QK; it costs 2.2% on the copy - the 16 B chunk
rotation destroys each 128 B line's store coalescing - and 1.5% on PV, so the
net is -0.4%.

Row 16 therefore closes as a drop, and it re-prices the row-10 follow-up: a
producer that fills this plane without the LSU/L1 cost is worth the 4.1% QK
delta at most, not the 16% the phase split first suggested. Two ways to chase
it if the area is ever re-opened: `cp.async.cg` for this copy only (row 9's
.cg was 13% slower on dense, but that was measured without the layout and
without the Q pollution in play), or a per-token TMA box into the swizzled
plane (row 8's dense form, which costs 64 boxes a page). Numbers of the run
above: 5 pairs 14933/15058, 14957/15056, 14791/14548, 14509/14373,
14223/14398 us, base/variant.

## Row 15 (accepted, unscheduled): the 512-dim NVFP4 record

Row 10 separated two things it changed at once: the *layout* of the sparse K
plane and the *engine* that fills it. The phase split then priced them
independently - the gather engine cost 5.4k cycles/page on the copy, and the
layout it wrote took 2.4k off QK. Row 16 keeps the layout and puts the
`cp.async` copy back, so the copy instruction count returns to base's (8 nope
chunks + 2 rope chunks per thread) and only the destination arithmetic changes:
each 16 B chunk lands at `k_row_ptr()` with a `(token & 7)` chunk rotation.

Why the layout is worth a row: base's plane is a 768 B token stride, so 32
lanes of a `b8` fragment read hit one bank set (768 B = 192 words, and 192 % 32
= 0); the XOR rotation spreads them. Row 10 measured 15.4k -> 13.0k cycle/page
of QK on a block that is 37% QK, so the Amdahl ceiling here is ~1.06x. The copy
pays only 10 extra integer ops per thread for the rotation, plus whatever the
destructed write pattern costs in store-coalescing - the halves of a 128 B line
that two different tokens write are no longer contiguous, and that is the part
that can sink the row.

Estimate 1.05-1.08x. Falsified if the copy rises by more than QK falls, or if
the copy holds and QK does not fall again. Same six gates, and the row is
judged on the sparse shape only.

## Row 15 (accepted, unscheduled): the 512-dim NVFP4 record

A capacity row, not a speed row. The dense cache is `V4` at 584 B/token (448
nope fp8 + 64 rope bf16 + 8 B scales); the fp4 analogue is `V41_FP4` at 288 B,
which is also 512-dim but quantizes the rope too. Three record shapes fall out
of the `KVCacheFormat` template at `D_QK=512` / nope 448:

| variant | data | scales | record | capacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rope fp4, tile 16, interleaved | 224 | 28 | 288 | 2.03x |
| **rope kept fp8** | 224 + 64 | 28 | 316 -> **320** | 1.83x |
| rope kept bf16 (today's 380 shape) | 224 + 128 | 28 | 380 -> **384** | 1.52x |

316 and 380 both fail TMA's `globalStrides % 16` check and pad to 320 / 384.
320 is the judgement point: the rope is the only part softmax does not
amortize over many tokens, so leaving it in fp8 keeps the fp4 error off the
whole tolerance budget. The 320 shape is also nearly free to build - `V41` is
already 512-dim and token-interleaved, and `KVCachePart`'s offsets are written
for exactly this; what changes is `D_FP4 = D_NOPE`, tile 16 and
`D_FP8 = D_ROPE`, plus the two hardcodes that would otherwise pin it
(`D_QK = IS_V32 || IS_NVFP4 ? 576 : 512` and the
`static_assert(BYTES_PER_TOKEN == 352)`).

`extra_kv` (816 B/token, a second full tensor) is a waypoint, not the endpoint,
because it keeps the fp8 cold plane: loading it sparsely by the selected set
bounds it at one 64-token page per selected position - 512 positions x 64 x
288 B = 9.4 MB/request, 226 MB over 24 requests, ~8% of a 4.8M-token pool
rather than the 40% a dense allocation costs. `extra_num_blocks` and
`extra_indices` already express it, so it costs a free list, an eviction
policy and a per-step fp8->fp4 page quantizer (~10.7 MB of traffic per step,
to be hidden), all on the host side. The single record removes all of that but
moves the quantizer onto the cache writer, so fp4 rounding applies to cold
tokens as well.

Gates: judged from an unquantized clone against the §5 fp8 numbers as a floor,
never relative to 288; the rope is gated separately by differencing a kept-fp8
and a kept-bf16 run. Capacity is booked on the pool arithmetic, outside the
gate. Row 7's 0.92x does not price this row's speed either - it says only that
an fp8 plane cannot pay through the mma on sm_120a, so the value has to come
from the bytes the copy and PV's B operand no longer move, not from QK.

## Non-goals


tcgen05, wgmma, cluster > 1, multicast, PDL, `f32x2`, 2 CTA/SM as a primary lever (worth ~1.05x and blocked behind row 3's register work).
